The 3-Day Ninja Intro

The playbook that turns shy cats into engaged ones — built for picky cats, works on the curious too.

Why slow wins.

Cats are predators with anxiety. Anything new is a threat first, a toy second. Rushing the intro is the #1 reason cat toys end up in the closet. Three days of low-pressure exposure flips the script — your cat's curiosity has to outweigh their suspicion. The playbook below is exactly how to do it.

The Playbook

1

Leave it near the food bowl 🍽️

Place the Ninja Cat next to where your cat eats. Don't move it. Don't play with it. Don't engage at all. Just let it sit there for the entire day.
Why this works: Pairing a new object with their existing safe space (food bowl = no threat) lets your cat sniff and inspect on their own terms. Curiosity builds when nobody is pushing.
2

Bait with fresh catnip 🌿

Rub fresh dried catnip on the gloves and the hood — generously. Use a fresh bag, not stale leaves. Walk away. Don't watch. Don't hover.
Why this works: The reward pairing (catnip = good feeling) attaches to the toy itself. Anxious cats won't engage with humans watching — solo investigation is critical.
3

Slow play only 🐾

Slip the puppet on. Slow, dragging movements only. Drag the sleeve across the floor like prey wounded and trying to escape. No sudden lunges. If they freeze, you stop too.
Why this works: Slow, low-to-the-ground movement triggers the hunt instinct (predator). Fast, erratic movement triggers the threat instinct (flight). You want the first response, not the second.
PRO TIP — FOR THE 30%

If catnip doesn't work, try silvervine.

About 30% of cats genetically don't respond to catnip — they're missing the receptor. If your cat sniffs the catnip and walks away unimpressed, switch to silvervine (Japanese matatabi). Studies show ~80% of catnip-immune cats respond strongly to silvervine.

Available on Amazon for $8-12. Try the powder form first — rub it on the puppet just like you would catnip. If your cat is one of the silvervine responders, you'll know within minutes.

If Day 3 doesn't work — try this

About 1 in 5 cats need more than 3 days, or a different angle. Work through these in order:

  • 1. Is your catnip fresh? Bagged catnip loses potency fast. A new bag (or silvervine, above) often fixes everything.
  • 2. Are you moving too fast? Cats respond to prey behavior — drag, don't lunge. Stop when they freeze. Resume slow.
  • 3. Is there too much going on? Quiet room. No kids, dogs, or TV. Cats need to focus to engage.
  • 4. Is your cat over 10? Senior cats often need 5-7 days, not 3. Extend the playbook — don't rush them.
  • 5. Does your cat play with anything? If they ignore all toys, the puppet isn't the problem. Worth a vet check for engagement / dental pain.
