THE 3-DAY NINJA INTRO

Slow is the cheat code.

Cats are predators with anxiety. Anything that lands in their territory is a threat first, a toy second. Three days of low-pressure introduction flips the script.

1

Leave it near the food bowl 🍽️

Place the Ninja Cat next to where your cat eats. Don't move it. Don't play with it. Don't engage.
Why: pairs the new object with safety (food). Lets curiosity build organically.
2

Bait it with fresh catnip 🌿

Rub fresh dried catnip (not the bagged stale stuff) on the gloves and hood. Walk away. Let them investigate alone.
Why: pairs the toy with reward without you hovering. Anxious cats won't engage with humans watching.
3

Slow play only 🐾

Slip the puppet on. Gentle, slow movements only. Drag the sleeve across the floor. Let them stalk. No sudden lunges.
Why: prey behavior triggers the hunt instinct. Sudden movement triggers the threat instinct. We want the first.
PRO TIP — SILVERVINE
About 30% of cats don't respond to catnip (genetic — they're missing the receptor). For those holdouts, try silvervine (Japanese matatabi). 8 out of 10 catnip-immune cats go nuts for it. Available on Amazon for $8-12. Worth the test before giving up.

That happens. Let's fix it.

About 1 in 5 cats need more time or a different angle. Try these in order:

  • Did you use fresh catnip? Bagged catnip loses potency fast. Try a new bag or — better — try silvervine (above).
  • Are you moving too fast? Cats respond to prey behavior. Drag, don't lunge. Stop when they freeze.
  • Is there too much going on? Try a quiet room with no kids, dogs, or TV. Cats need to focus.
  • Is your cat over 10? Older cats take 5-7 days, not 3. Extend the playbook and don't rush.
  • Does your cat play with anything else? If they ignore all toys, your puppet isn't the problem — talk to your vet about engagement.
